Kontuur / Redesign / Calendar

The calendar shows what is scheduled. It never shows what isn’t.

Postflow already computes when each client should publish — best_time_json, posts_per_week and pickNextOpenSlot all exist, and /review uses them. The calendar page queries id, name, contact_email and knows none of it. So it can render occupancy and never deficit — which means it cannot answer the one question an agency opens it to ask: who is about to go dark?

01

What’s actually wrong

Six specialists read every file in src/features/calendar/ plus the publishing pipeline around it. Two independent critics then tried to knock the findings down. These survived. They fall into three groups: gestures that are decoration, dates that are wrong, and a surface built before the design system existed.

Correctness

Five different opinions about what day it is

Posts are bucketed by slicing the raw UTC string (scheduled_at.slice(0,10)), “today” is resolved in the browser’s zone, the month is resolved in the agency’s zone, and the schedule form prefills its date in UTC beside its time in local. Reopen a post and press Update and it moves a day. Posts render on the wrong cell.

Dead gesture

Drag-and-drop that has no drag

day-cell.tsx implements the complete HTML5 drop protocol. Nothing in the app sets draggable — the only draggable in the repo is inside the Konva canvas editor, which is a different system entirely. The only thing droppable on a day is stray selected text, which the handler forwards to a real updatePost.

Unreachable

The third post on any day does not exist

MAX_VISIBLE = 2, the “+N” badge is an inert <div>, and the grid is pinned to viewport height under overflow: hidden. Post three is unreachable by mouse, keyboard and screen reader. The two shown are the two most recently created, not the two earliest.

Lie

Day cells advertise a click that does nothing

Every cell carries cursor: pointer, a hover shadow the Resting Surface Rule forbids, and an onClick wired to a literal noop. Three of the calendar’s four scheduling gestures are costume.

Identity

Clients change colour when you filter

DESIGN.md §240 requires client hue to be hashed from the client’s name, so they wear the same colour on their avatar, dot and calendar pill forever. month-grid.tsx instead indexes the palette by position in the currently-visible sorted set. Filter, and everyone’s colour shifts.

Scope

A 1,085-line modal with the date field last

ScheduleCard carries nine responsibilities — caption editing, carousel slides, AI visual generation, the Konva editor, quality scores, validation, publish-now, approval and scheduling. Its date input sits at the bottom of the scroll column, below six slide editors, in a dialog whose primary button says Schedule.

System

The last surface predating Kontuur

96 inline style={{}} objects across nine components, raw '#fff', hardcoded radii and rgba() hairlines, hover states mutated imperatively through onMouseEnter. DESIGN.md: “Components use classes, never inline style objects.”

Access

No keyboard path through the grid at all

Day cells are bare <div onClick> — no role, no tabIndex, no key handling, no role="grid", no roving tabindex. Changing month replaces 42 cells in silence. At 375px the columns are 43.6px wide and overflow: hidden destroys the excess rather than scrolling it.

02

The proposal

The week becomes the working unit — the unit the approval batch, posts_per_week and best_time_json already speak. Month demotes to a coverage overview. Every slot a client’s cadence implies but nothing fills is drawn in --hatch, the token DESIGN.md already reserves for absence, and clicking one is how you place a post. The backlog stops being a floating panel over the grid and becomes furniture beside it.

03

Five calls I need you to make

Everything above is settled — the bugs are bugs and the token work is owed. These five are genuine forks where a different answer changes what gets built. My recommendation is on the left.

01What is the calendar’s working unit?
Recommend Week is the default; month becomes a coverage overview. Week is already the product’s native unit everywhere but here: the approval batch is week-scoped, posts_per_week is the target, best_time_json expands to a week. A month cell is a ~90×100px budget that legibly holds two marks — which is exactly why MAX_VISIBLE = 2 and the broken “+N” exist at all. Month stays, because “is November thin?” is a real question; it just stops being where you place work.
Alternative A client-rows × days matrix as the primary view. One row per client, seven day columns. Genuinely the strongest idea in the research: it moves client identity from the hue channel (capacity ~6, fails for colour-blind users, illegible at 5px) to the position channel, which is unbounded — and a cadence gap becomes a literal hole in a row. I’m deferring rather than rejecting it: it’s a third data structure, and it only beats week view past roughly six simultaneous clients.
02Where do open slots come from?
Recommend Derive them from what already exists. No migration. best_time_json + posts_per_week + pickNextOpenSlot are all built and already consumed by /review. Add two fields to the calendar’s query, promote the slot picker to src/lib/scheduling/, and the ghost layer exists. Where a client has no best-time data yet, it degrades to nothing rather than to a guess.
Alternative A real posting_slots table you edit per client. Buffer’s model, and the better long-term primitive — agencies will eventually want to pin “Acme posts Tue/Thu 09:00” by hand rather than accept what Instagram’s data infers. But it’s a migration, a settings surface, onboarding seeding and reconciliation against best_time_json. Worth building after someone has used derived slots. Note: the existing posting_schedules table is not this — it governs when posts are generated, not when they publish.
03Does the calendar keep editing post content?
Recommend No. It edits when and whether. Content goes back to review. The 1,085-line modal becomes a small Place dialog. Captions, slides, visual generation, the Konva editor and quality all leave — review already owns those surfaces and does them better. Bonus: the closed modal currently ships zod, CarouselSlides and CanvasEditor into /calendar’s first load (807 KB route JS today). The catch: /review only queries pending_review posts and there is no /posts/[id] route — so the destination has to be built before anything is deleted.
Alternative Keep one modal, but decompose it and put the date first. Cheaper, no new route, and preserves “fix a typo without leaving the calendar” — which you may genuinely use daily. It leaves the bundle problem, and it keeps two content editors in the product that will drift apart. Tell me if you edit captions from the calendar in practice. That single fact decides this one.
04Drag first, or command first?
Recommend One movePost command; drag added last as an accelerator on it. WCAG 2.5.7 requires a non-dragging single-pointer path regardless, so the command has to exist either way. Building it first makes drag nearly free; building drag first guarantees two write paths that drift — they already have, which is why handleDrop hardcodes noon while the modal passes a real timestamp. One command is also one place to put optimistic apply, rollback and Undo.
Alternative Fix drag first — it’s what people expect of a calendar. Fair, and it’s the gesture that feels like a calendar. But nothing depends on the current implementation because it has never worked, so there is no migration pressure — and shipping drag onto a grid whose date handling is still wrong just moves posts to the wrong day faster.
05Should the week actions follow the week on screen?
Recommend Yes — and label them with the week they will send. Today “Copy link” and “Email client” act on the real wall-clock week no matter what month you’re looking at — navigate to September, click Email, and August goes out. Once week view exists the fix is obvious: the rail follows the viewed week and says so. Both the client-side window and createApprovalBatch’s server-side window must move together, or the token covers a different post set than the button promised.
Alternative Stay pinned to the current week, labelled explicitly. “Send week of Mon 3 Aug” regardless of what you’re viewing. Safer against misfires, and it matches how approvals actually work — you almost always send the upcoming week. Less flexible if you ever want to send a client two weeks ahead.
04

How it ships

Six phases, each shippable on its own, ordered so the highest user value lands first. No database migration is needed for any of it — publish_error, publish_attempts, published_at and topic_summary all already exist in the generated types. No new library either; the existing HTML5 drag API is sufficient once it has a source.

1 Truth pass The grid stops lying. One timezone, one date key, every post reachable, client colour stable. No visual redesign — this is mostly deletion, and it is the highest value per line in the plan.
  • Every date resolves through the agency zone, on both read and write — there is currently no zoned wall-clock→instant helper at all, and every write path needs one before any new writer exists.
  • Day buckets sort by scheduled_at, not by creation order.
  • Grid unpins from viewport height; “+N” becomes a real button opening a day popover.
  • Client hue hashes off the client’s name — which also deletes the unstable closure that was silently defeating memo(DayCell) on all 42 cells.
  • The dead drop chain, the noop day click and the affordances advertising them are removed. Nothing that lies stays.
  • Riding along: the failed-publish re-arm fix (reset publish_attempts, publish_error and publish_claimed_at, as a dedicated action rather than widening the generic update whitelist), and the bundle win — adapt validation server-side and lazy-load the canvas editor. Both are independent of the redesign and owed regardless.
Ships alone
2 Ghost slots on the grid you already have The calendar starts showing the target, not just the occupancy. Deliberately lands before the week view, because it is the highest-leverage change in the plan and it needs no new grid.
  • Fetch the cadence the calendar has never seen; promote the slot picker to shared code and fix its timezone handling in the same commit as its test.
  • Ghost slots render in --hatch. Clicking one is what finally gives the day click something honest to do.
  • The pill is rebuilt against tokens: hashed monogram, topic_summary instead of the pillar name, status as form rather than hue, and a real accessible label.
  • The single movePost command lands here, behind a per-pill action menu.
Ships alone
3 Week view and keyboard Seven full-height columns, time-ordered, every post visible — plus the Week | Month tab rail, reusing the existing component unchanged.
  • Full ARIA grid semantics, roving tabindex and arrow-key navigation — done once, here, after the grid’s markup has settled. Doing it in phase 2 would mean doing it twice.
  • An agenda fallback below the mobile breakpoint. At 375px the current grid gives 43.6px columns and destroys the overflow rather than scrolling it, which also fails reflow at 200% desktop zoom.
Ships alone
4 Docked queue rail The backlog becomes furniture beside the grid instead of an overlay covering it — and stops disagreeing with itself about how many posts are in it.
  • Collapsible to a counted spine, correctly inert when collapsed, with the grid’s client filter applied and stated.
  • Today the panel header shows an unfiltered count and the sort row shows a filtered one, 40px apart. One count: “7 of 40”.
  • The floating action button goes away; so does the pinned footer band spending fixed vertical space on a static sentence.
Ships alone
5 Liveness and the failure lane The calendar tells the truth about what already happened. Today it is frozen at page load: the cron publishes and client approvals land, and an open tab never knows.
  • Refresh on focus and after every mutation, hard-gated against clobbering in-flight edits.
  • Failed posts sort to the top of their day with the reason on the pill, and the dashboard’s “needs a human” card deep-links to the post rather than to a bare /calendar.
  • Pending approval finally renders — today it falls through every branch — including whether the 72-hour link has quietly expired.
  • Per-post pending state instead of one shared saving flag, so a caption autosave stops disabling the Schedule button and a failed schedule stops closing the dialog as though it worked.
Ships alone
6 Drag, bulk moves, and bounded queries Speed. Drag becomes a real accelerator on the command from phase 2, and a slipped launch week stops costing forty dialog round-trips.
  • Typed drag payload, a real drag ghost, and time-of-day preserved on a day-drop — never the hardcoded noon.
  • Optimistic apply with visible rollback and an Undo toast on every move. Drag is a low-precision gesture; cheap reversal beats any amount of hover affordance.
  • Range-select and “shift selected by ±N days”, through the existing batchSchedulePosts action rather than forty sequential writes.
  • View, week and client filter move into the URL — which is what finally lets the server stop fetching every post the agency has ever produced, with full slides, validation and image rows, on every load.
Ships alone
05

Still open

01Per-client timezones. Everything resolves to one agency zone. An agency with clients in different markets can’t share one. If per-client zones are coming, slot times should be stored client-local now rather than migrated later.
02Nine clients. The identity palette holds eight hues. Past eight the answer is monogram-plus-desaturation rather than cycling — but is a nine-client roster a real near-term case?
03Upstream volume. The page fetches five statuses, so drafts and pending-review posts are invisible and the calendar reads emptier than the pipeline actually is. Should the header say “12 more upstream”, or does that belong entirely to review?
04Thumbnails. Instagram planners lean hard on a visual grid. Postflow generates the imagery already. Worth a density control — dots / pills / thumbnails — or does week view make month density a non-problem?
