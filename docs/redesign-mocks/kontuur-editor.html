Kontuur/ Redesign/ Canvas editor

An editor with one panel is a settings screen with a picture in it.

Today every verb in the editor lives in one 300px column on the right — text layers, fonts, elements, cutouts, scrim and background, in a fixed vertical stack that is not selection-driven at all: the sections are simply always there, and only the text block and one element sub-block appear and vanish. This is the consumer-editor anatomy instead: a section rail for what you can add, a flyout for the section you are in, and a contextual toolbar that only ever shows the verbs the current selection can take.

Two full states below — normal editing and inpaint — plus the three toolbars side by side, because the whole proposal rests on them being different. Nothing here is interactive: it is drawn, not wired. Georgia stands in for Instrument Serif and the system sans for Geist, since neither font can load in this file.

Under about 1180px the flyout collapses to an overlay above the canvas and the rail keeps its width — the sheet is the one thing that must not shrink, because everything else in the screen exists to change it. Selection

One bar, three contents

This is the whole proposal in three strips. Today all of these controls are present at once in the right-hand column, greyed or inert when they do not apply. Here the bar holds only what the selection can take, and names what it is bound to at the right end — so an empty-looking bar is never ambiguous.

96 Headline · text
Text selected

Family, size, weight, alignment, colour, leading, rotation — then the three treatments the composition engine actually ships: uppercase, real italics, and the marker highlight, which carries its own Marker tint when it is on rather than a checkbox. Alignment is icons, not the letters L C R the panel uses today: three capitals in a row are a legibility trap at 25px and they do not survive translation. The size stepper is a stepper because 96 → 92 is a nudge, not a form entry. The family list is the document’s own twenty — font-library.ts, Playfair through Caveat — and it keeps hiding Latin-only faces when the layer carries Cyrillic. Kontuur’s Instrument Serif is chrome and never appears in it.

84% Fjord table · image
Element selected

Opacity gets a slider with its number beside it, because it is the one value here people scrub rather than type. Order is two verbs — Forward and Back, today’s Bring forward / Send backward — not a z-index field. The three consequential verbs (Set as background, Remove background, Erase parts) keep their words: each changes the document in a way no icon reliably predicts, and this is the bar where a wrong guess costs an undo. Two open items. The flips are new — nothing in the editor flips an element today. And the panel’s In front of text checkbox has no home here yet; it is a third z-order fact that Forward / Back cannot express, so it either becomes a toggle in this bar or the row order in Layers absorbs it.

Scrim 26% Nothing selected — these act on the background
Nothing selected

The state that has no home today. Click the workspace and the bar becomes the background’s: move the crop, set the legibility scrim, open inpainting, swap the image outright. It is also the honest place for AI repair — it acts on the backdrop, so it belongs where the backdrop’s verbs are rather than in a panel three sections away. The right end says so in words instead of leaving a near-empty bar to be read as a bug. Two more arrive conditionally, exactly as they do now: the background Zoom · 1.40× slider and Reset crop, which only exists once a crop transform does. Scrim keeps its detail behind the control — coverage (bottom band or full canvas) and colour open from it rather than spending four slots in the bar. Replace image lives outside the editor today, on the review column; it belongs here, next to the thing it replaces.

State two

Inpaint is a mode, and admits it

Brushing over a picture to describe what should replace it is not a property, and it cannot live in a panel beside sliders that still look clickable. It takes the screen: the document drops to 35% so the mask is the only thing at full strength, the contextual toolbar is replaced by the mode’s own bar on a --wash ground, the rail and flyout go inert, and the way out is stated over the canvas rather than remembered.

Recoloured. Moves

What each decision buys

Structure

Adding and changing are different questions

The rail answers “what can I put here”; the toolbar answers “what can I do to this”. One column answering both is why every control has to be present at once, and why the panel scrolls past the thing you came for.

The point

The toolbar names its subject

Every variant ends with what it is bound to — Headline · text, Fjord table · image, or the sentence for nothing. A context-sensitive bar with no subject line is the fastest way to make people believe controls have disappeared.

Palette

One lime answer: Save

The commitment in the header, carrying Pine Deep over an inset edge at 45%. The rail’s active section is Wash and Deep Pine, and the dock’s Generate is a Deep Pine primary. Two lime plates on one screen and neither reads as the thing that ends the session.

Furniture

Zoom leaves the panel

It is a view control, not a document property, and it belongs where the eye already is: over the bottom-right of the workspace, small, on Surface with --sh-pop. It is also the only floating object in the column, which is what makes the shadow legible as meaning.

New

The dock is the only genuinely new capability

There is no background generation inside the editor at all today: the three AI verbs it has are Recraft vectors, BiRefNet cutouts and gpt-image inpainting, and regenerating a visual means leaving for the review column, where it is single-shot and destructive — no candidates, no picker, no way back but undo. Docked under the canvas, the prompt and the last three results sit beside the thing you are judging them against.

Layers

Two lists become one

Text layers and elements are separate lists today, ordered opposite ways — text in document order, elements reversed so the topmost reads first — and only one of them can reorder. The rail’s Layers section is where they reconcile into a single stack, which is also the only place In front of text stops being a checkbox and becomes a position.

Deviation

Two things the system does not have

A guide colour. Snap guides are dashed --danger — the nearest token, chosen because it never appears elsewhere over artwork; it is transient chrome, and it never touches a surface where clay means error. A sheet radius. The canvas is square-cornered rather than any step of the 4/8/10/14/20 scale, because it is a 1080×1350 document and a rounded corner there would be a claim about what publishes.

Owed

Every icon-only control needs its word

Alignment, flips, reorder, delete and the type toggles are icon-only by design and therefore owe aria-label plus a tooltip, and the toolbar owes role="toolbar" with roving focus. Drawn here, they are stated in the markup; in code they are the acceptance criteria.

Reuse

The canvas keeps its engine

Nothing above changes the Konva document model, the cutout toolkit, or how a composition is rendered — the 35% dim, the Escape ladder and the exclusive modes already work exactly as drawn. This is chrome: what wraps the stage, and which verb is reachable when. One tidy-up rides along: the top bar’s undo and redo are a bespoke 28px inline-styled button today while the zoom cluster uses TOOL_ROW at 29px, so the same control has two sizes in one screen. Everything here is TOOL_ROW.

Before code

Four calls I need from you

The anatomy is the thing to approve or reject as a whole. These four are the places where I could reasonably have drawn it the other way. My answer is on the record next to each.

01 Does the flyout stay open, or collapse after you place something? Stays open, and remembers. Drawn open at 280px it costs a quarter of the horizontal budget, which is why the sheet is at 42% rather than 55%. Auto-collapse buys the canvas back but makes the panel feel like it is being taken away mid-task — and people place several elements in a row far more often than one. The chevron in the panel header is the manual answer, and the state should persist per user rather than reset every open.
02 Is Save the lime, or is it the dock’s Generate? Save. The Standing Place Rule says lime marks the commitment in the header, and on /generate that is exactly what it marks. Here the commitment is the one that ends the session and writes the document; generating a background is a step inside it, however expensive it feels. If you’d rather the lime went to Generate, then Save becomes a Deep Pine primary and the header carries no lime at all — what it must not do is carry both.
03 Should AI repair live in the no-selection toolbar, or in the rail’s AI section? The toolbar, with an entry from the rail too. It acts on the backdrop, so its home is where the backdrop’s other verbs are. But “AI” is where people will look first, and a section that lists the AI verbs and routes into the same mode costs nothing. The risk to avoid is two different inpaint entry points with different state — one mode, two doors.
04 Full-screen, or docked inside the app shell? Full-screen, as today. The rail plus flyout plus a legible 4:5 sheet needs 1280px before the app’s own 240px sidebar is counted, and an editor is a place you go rather than a page you browse. That does mean the top bar owes a real way back — the chevron and Cancel both return to the post, and unsaved work has to be caught there rather than silently discarded.

Approve the anatomy and the next wave is chrome only: extract the toolbar variants behind a selection type, move the existing controls into them unchanged, add the rail and the flyout around the stage, and dock the prompt bar. The Konva layer, the cutout toolkit and the saved document stay exactly where they are.
