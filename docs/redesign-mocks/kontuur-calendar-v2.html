Kontuur/ Redesign/ Calendar

A calendar that can say who is about to go dark.

The month grid shows occupancy and nothing else — two pills a day, an inert “+N”, a click wired to noop, and every route into one 952-line modal. This is the working screen instead: the week as the unit, the slots a client’s cadence implies drawn in --hatch whether or not anything fills them, and the backlog docked beside the grid rather than covering it.

Everything below is drawn from one data model, so the three views cannot disagree with each other. Times are Europe/Sofia. Georgia stands in for Instrument Serif and the system sans for Geist — neither font can load here.

What each decision buys

Structure

Week is the working unit

Seven full-height columns, time-ordered, every post reachable. The week is already the product’s native unit everywhere else — the approval batch is week-scoped, posts_per_week is the target, best_time_json expands to a week. A month cell is a ~90×100px budget that legibly holds two marks, which is precisely why MAX_VISIBLE = 2 exists.

The point

Absence is drawn, not inferred

A hatched slot is what the client’s cadence implies and nothing fills. It is derived from data the app already computes and /review already consumes — no migration, no new table. Where a client has no best-time data it degrades to nothing rather than to a guess.

Settled

The dialog stays exactly as it is

Caption, visual, regenerate, the Konva editor, quality, source, platform, publish-now, delete — all of it. It already shows everything the decision needs, and replacing it would have cost a rebuild to arrive somewhere worse. The slot click opens ScheduleCard with the client, date and time filled in. The 807 KB route stays; lazy-loading the canvas editor is still worth doing on its own, and is independent of this.

One change

The stepper scopes to the client

Everything else is reused verbatim. But the header's 1 of 48 walks every unscheduled post in the agency, and from Билков Дом’s Thursday 10:00 most of those cannot go in it. Entered from a slot it reads 1 of 2 and steps only that client’s backlog; entered from the queue it behaves as it does today. Scheduling also becomes the primary button, because the date is already filled — publish-now demotes.

Furniture

The backlog stops covering the grid

The floating action button and the 360px slide-over go; the queue docks on the right, collapses to a counted spine, and states its own filter. Hovering an item lights the slots that client can actually take — the two halves of the screen finally know about each other.

Correctness

One opinion about what day it is

Today the day bucket is a substring of a UTC string, “today” is the browser’s, the month is the agency’s, and the schedule form prefills a UTC date beside a local time. Every date here resolves through the agency zone on read and write, which is a prerequisite for any new writer, not a polish item.

Identity

Client colour stops moving

month-grid.tsx indexes the palette by position in the currently-visible sorted set, so filtering repaints everyone. Hashing off the name — which DESIGN.md §240 already requires — also deletes the unstable closure silently defeating memo(DayCell) on all 42 cells.

Detail

Three objects, close up

The card

Monogram, time, topic_summary — not the pillar name the current pill shows. Status is carried by form (fill and edge) before it is carried by hue, so it survives a colour-blind reading and a greyscale print.

The ghost slot

Rest, lit, and past. Lit is what a queue item’s hover does — the slot answers back with how many waiting posts fit. A slot in the past keeps its place in amber: it is a record of a miss, not a task you can still do.

The coverage strip

DESIGN.md’s signature component, finally on the surface it was designed for. It moves client identity off the hue channel — capacity ~6, illegible at 5px — and onto position, which is unbounded. A cadence gap becomes a literal hole in a row.

Small

Below the breakpoint, an agenda

At 375px the current grid gives 43.6px columns and overflow: hidden destroys the excess rather than scrolling it — which also fails reflow at 200% desktop zoom. Seven columns do not survive a phone, so below md the week becomes one day at a time.

Sorting it to the top was the first thing I drew and DESIGN.md talked me out of it — a state signal must not be carried by position, and the clay tint says “needs you” wherever the card sits. On the phone the day’s dot carries it instead, so a failure is findable without opening anything.

Before code

Four calls I need from you

The bugs are bugs and the token work is owed either way. These four change what gets built. My answer is on the record next to each.

01 Does Clients earn a place as a third view? I think yes, and cheaply. It reads the same data the week view already has — no extra query, no new state — and it is the only view that answers “who is about to go dark” without you counting. The draft deferred it on the grounds that it only beats week view past about six clients. Having drawn it, I disagree: at five clients it is already the faster read. If you’d rather keep the rail to two tabs, the alternative is to fold it into the header as a strip that opens on click.
02 Do you ever edit a caption from the calendar? — Answered: yes Not whether you can see it — you can, in full. The question is whether you need to change it without leaving. If you don’t, the preview stays read-only, the editing half of the 952-line modal leaves, and most of the route’s 807 KB goes with it. If you do, the peek keeps a “Fix caption” verb and we owe the /posts/[id] route it opens into — because /review only queries pending_review posts today, so that destination does not yet exist for a scheduled one.
03 Should “Send week for approval” follow the week on screen? Yes, and labelled with what it will send. Today Copy link and Email client act on the real wall-clock week no matter what you’re looking at — navigate to September, click Email, and August goes out. The mock labels both “w/c 3 Aug” and moves them with the arrows. The catch is that the client-side window and createApprovalBatch’s server-side window must move together, or the token covers a different set of posts than the button promised.
04 Derived slots, or a real posting_slots table? Derived first. best_time_json + posts_per_week + pickNextOpenSlot are built and already consumed by /review; promoting the picker to src/lib/scheduling/ and adding two fields to the calendar’s query is the whole cost. A real table is the better long-term primitive — agencies will want to pin “Acme posts Tue/Thu 09:00” by hand — but it is a migration, a settings surface and reconciliation against inferred times. Worth building after someone has lived with derived slots for a month. (Note: the existing posting_schedules table is not this — it governs when posts are generated, not when they publish.)

Say yes to the shape and I’ll turn the draft’s six phases into a real implementation plan against these files, with the truth pass (timezone, sort order, client hue, the dead drop chain) ordered first because everything else writes dates through it.
