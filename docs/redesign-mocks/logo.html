The wordmark, alone

No mark beside the name. The name set in the brand serif is the logo — the way direction-01 already has it, and the way the email header already reads. Below: the lockup at the sizes it actually appears, then the two places that cannot fit a word and need an answer.

One word, one full stop

Instrument Serif italic, lowercase, with the full stop carrying the colour: Deep Pine on light grounds, lime on Pine Deep. That inversion is not decoration — Living Green as a word on paper measures 3.38:1 and fails, and lime on paper is 1.35:1 and fails harder. The dot is the only place colour appears, so it is the only place the rule has to hold.

Unplated, and where the lime goes

You asked for the unplated mark with lime as an accent, and that runs into the one rule the palette is strictest about: lime measures 1.35:1 on paper, so a lime full stop on white is very nearly invisible — DESIGN.md forbids lime as "a line, a dot, or a word on a light surface" by name. What it does allow is lime as a fill carrying ink, which is the marker treatment already in the app. Both readings are below, plus the literal lime dot so you can see the problem rather than take my word for it.

What this changes, and the one thing to decide

Decide: where the lime goes

I would take B — the dot as a lime tile. It is the closest honest reading of what you asked for: the full stop is the lime, it just carries ink instead of being ink. It survives at 16px, it needs no plate, and it keeps the one-mark restraint of the wordmark. A is the bolder answer and the more distinctive one at large sizes, but a marker swipe behind a single letter starts to read as a highlight rather than a logo once it is small.

C is shown to be rejected. Look at it on this near-white ground: the full stop all but disappears. That is the 1.35:1 the palette warns about, and it gets worse on pure white, which is where most of the app's cards sit.

One consequence of going unplated either way: on the Pine Deep rail the mark inverts to white with a plain lime dot, because on dark the relationship flips and lime becomes legible ink at 10.87:1. So the lockup has two forms rather than one. That is a small ongoing cost the plated versions did not have.

There are three different marks in the codebase today

  • wordmark.tsx — a lime plate carrying a sans k, beside the name.
  • src/app/icon.svg — a geometric lime k on Pine Deep, built from rectangles and polygons. Nothing else in the app looks like it.
  • public/favicon.ico — whatever was there before either of those.

All three should become the one compact form, and the old ones deleted rather than left alongside.

The code change is small

wordmark.tsx is already the single source — sidebar, auth panel, generate chrome, onboarding, marketing nav and footer all read from it. The plate span comes out, markOnly starts rendering the compact k instead of the plate, and icon.svg is redrawn to match. Six call sites, no changes needed at any of them.

One honest caveat about the serif

A logo that is pure type depends entirely on the typeface being right. Instrument Serif is a free Google font, so the wordmark is not ownable the way a drawn mark would be — a competitor could set their name identically. That is a real trade for the simplicity you asked for, and worth knowing rather than discovering later. Drawing custom letterforms from it later is the usual fix, and it does not block anything now.
