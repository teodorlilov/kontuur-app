Kontuur · redesign

One atom, many arrangements

Every screen in this product is the same object — a post — arranged by a different question. Get the post right and the surfaces stop arguing with each other.

The principle

The last three mocks failed for one reason: they described posts instead of showing them. A roster of clients with counts, a table of weeks, a grid of tiles — all of them are metadata about the work, and the work itself never appeared. Every tool in this category learned the same lesson: Planable, Later and Buffer all put the post on screen and let structure fall out of that.

So the system has exactly one repeated object. A surface is not a new design — it is the post card at a chosen density, arranged by day, by state, or by result. That is what makes the tabs feel like one product.

But not every surface is a content surface. Kontuur has two families, and mixing them is what made the last mock wrong. Content surfaces carry the atom: Dashboard, Calendar, Review, Generate, Ideas, Analytics, the approval portal. Configuration surfaces deliberately do not: Clients, client detail, Settings. Posts already live in three places; a fourth would only make "where do I go to see content" ambiguous.

Clients answers a different question entirely — is this brand set up to produce good work, and is it still working? That is readiness, not content. And it is where the product's own claim is at risk: a client source that has quietly stopped fetching degrades source-grounded generation, and today nothing anywhere tells you.

Micro — presence only

The week strip is not a chart — it is seven post cards shrunk to the point where only existence survives. Filled, scheduled, empty. This is why the strip already feels native: it was the atom all along.

The shell — cross-client is the point

The research says agencies work one brand at a time behind a workspace switcher. We should not copy that. Kontuur is built for someone running eight brands at once, and the hardest thing they do is hold all eight in their head — so the cross-client view is the product's job, not a compromise on the way to a single-client view.

So Clients stays a real destination, and client detail keeps its own surface. What the scope control adds is a filter, not a mode: stay across everything by default, narrow when you need to. Depth per brand lives where it already belongs — client detail for setup and voice, Analytics for performance.

No surface invents a new pattern. Settings is the only screen with no post in it, and it should look like it — plain, quiet, obviously a different kind of place.

One fact, one home

No number appears in two places. The critique found "5" on screen four times at once — sidebar badge, stat card, panel header and a shortcut tile — which is how a product starts feeling like a report about itself.

The line that makes this workable is granularity, not subject. The Dashboard is the single source of truth for everything across all clients: the aggregate, the comparison between brands, the exceptions. Every other tab owns the detail and the actions inside its own domain. The same subject can appear at both levels — that is a summary and its detail, not a duplicate — but the aggregate exists in exactly one place, and so does the list.

DashboardAll clientsThe whole book of business, now: how much of the week is filled across every brand, which brands are behind, what is failing or blocked. Nothing here is per-post and nothing is editable — it hands off.
CalendarThe scheduleThe posts themselves, placed in time. Drag to move. The only surface that shows an individual scheduled post.
ReviewThe queueDrafts awaiting a decision, with source and scores. Approve and undo happen here and nowhere else.
ClientsReadinessConfiguration only — voice, sources and their fetch health, connection, schedule, identity. No counts, no coverage, no posts.
AnalyticsPerformanceWhat happened over time. The Dashboard owns the operational present; Analytics owns the measured past. That is the line between them.
IdeasClient requestsWhat the client asked for, and what became of it.

Two consequences worth stating plainly. Clients loses every metric — the week strip, the post counts, the coverage. Those are cross-client data and belong to the Dashboard; Clients keeps only whether a brand is correctly set up. And the Dashboard stops restating lists: it carries the aggregate and the exception, then hands off. A count in the nav is a pointer, not ownership.

The open piece is the comparison. "Which brands are behind" is real cross-client data with no other home, and it needs a form. A row-per-client ledger is the obvious answer and it is the one you have already rejected twice on looks. That visual is the next decision, not a settled part of this vision.

Ground, and how it moves

The contour field is the ground of every surface. Kontuur means contour, and a week of scheduled content is terrain — ridges where the calendar is full, flats where it is open. A card is a clearing: opaque, hairline edge, contour lines stopping at its boundary. Depth comes from interrupting the pattern, not from a shadow.

Three movements, each carrying a meaning rather than decorating one: the week fills in as bars rise from the baseline on load; a row opens into a card when you look closer; the terrain rises where you point, so attention reads as elevation. Everything stops under prefers-reduced-motion.

What this needs from you

  • Does the atom hold? If the post card is right, every surface below follows. If it is wrong, everything inherits the mistake — so this is the decision worth spending time on.
  • Provenance is the anti-copy move. Source line and voice/language scores on the card are things Planable, Later and Buffer structurally cannot show, because they do not generate grounded content. If we only borrow their layouts we ship a worse version of their product; this is where we stop borrowing.
  • Cross-client is the default, not a compromise. Scope narrows the view; it never becomes the only way in. Clients and client detail stay real destinations, and Analytics owns per-brand depth.
  • Source health is the gap worth closing. client_sources already stores last_fetch_status and last_fetch_error, and nothing in the product surfaces them. A source that quietly stopped fetching weakens source-grounded generation — the claim the whole product rests on — without anyone being told.
  • Imagery leads, and Kontuur already generates it. The visuals here are your own mock assets. Posts without a generated image need a defined fallback — currently they would render as an empty frame.
  • Still unresolved: warm paper versus near-white ground, --clay-deep entering the palette, and the overused-font suppression for Geist and Instrument Serif.
